Football terms & units

Snap = the play starts. Release = the ball is thrown. A route = one receiver’s run. yd = yards; 2 yards ≈ 1.83 metres. s = seconds.

Play / route
A play is one action by the teams. A route is one receiver’s run within that play; several receivers can run routes at once.
Separation / spacing
The distance from the receiver to the closest defender. The closest defender can change as players move.
Window
Here, a stretch of time with a gap above our distance threshold. It does not mean a clear path for throwing the ball.
Line of scrimmage / downfield
The line of scrimmage is where the ball starts the play. Downfield means beyond that line, toward the end the offense is attacking.
Down & distance
The offense normally has four attempts (downs) to gain 10 yards for a new set. “3rd & 8” means third attempt, with 8 yards still needed. Goal to go means the goal line is the target.
Alignment
Where the receiver starts. Wide means near the sideline; Slot means farther inside. HB means halfback and TE means tight end, two player roles.
Coverage
The defense’s plan for guarding receivers or areas of the field. Labels such as Cover-1 and Cover-2 identify different plans; you can leave this filter on All.
Film review / dropback
Film review means watching game video. A dropback is the quarterback’s movement back to prepare a pass; longer plays allow more time for gaps to appear.
What counts as an earlier gap?

All five rules must hold:

More than 2 yardsfrom the closest defender (about 1.83 metres)
At least 0.5 secondswith that gap continuously
After 1.0 secondfrom the start of the play (snap)
At least 2 yards forwardbeyond where the ball started (downfield)
Until 0.3 secondsbefore the ball is thrown (release)

Speed, gap change & the first-down line

Speed is recorded for this receiver at this frame, not a rating of how fast the player usually is. Opening / closing compares the nearest gap now with 0.3 seconds earlier; the closest opponent can change. Rates within ±0.2 yd/s display as steady, and the first 0.3 seconds have no trend yet.

The blue dashed line marks the yards needed for a new set of downs, or the goal line on goal-to-go plays. Being beyond it does not prove a pass, catch or conversion was available. These context cues do not alter the spacing score or predict ball flight.

Read the method

Definitions, exclusions and limits

__ROUTE_COUNT__ eligible runs · __PLAY_COUNT__ pass plays. Reproducible analysis; football mentor review pending.

Which routes are included?

Routes must have complete snap-to-release tracking, all 11 defenders, validated 10 Hz timing, finite coordinates, a known line of scrimmage, an in-bounds receiver track, a WR/TE/RB pass-route role, at least 1.8 seconds observed and a receiver reaching at least 2 yards downfield. Only completed, incomplete and intercepted pass outcomes are included. All route runners are evaluated; no target label or route-type label is assumed.

How are windows measured?

Separation is the distance to the nearest of all 11 defenders at the same frame, including pass rushers. The nearest player can change. Spacing must be strictly greater than 2 yards, from 1 second after the snap through 0.3 seconds before release, while at least 2 yards downfield. Six consecutive 10 Hz samples span 0.5 seconds. Durations use observed endpoints; missing, ineligible or below-threshold frames break windows. No interpolation is used.

What does spacing leave out?

Nearest-defender distance omits passing lanes, leverage, coverage responsibility, route intent and quarterback vision. Alignment and coverage strata do not remove those limitations. The result does not identify missed reads, receiver quality or causal effects. Use the queue to prioritise film review, then assess whether the space was usable.

How are uncertainty and reproduction handled?

Intervals use 2,000 whole-game bootstrap resamples with seed 20261021. They describe game-cluster variability in this selected sample, not tracking accuracy or future-season generalisation. Source, exclusions, definitions and exact IDs are recorded in analysis.json, event_audit.json and routes.csv. Inputs are pinned and checksum-verified.
